Predicted entrance-exam score and residual: logistic models

Grade-8 cohorts 2017 and 2019 · 233,787 exam takers · Average marginal effects

The observed math–verbal entrance-exam composite is split into its baseline prediction, bi, and its residual, gi = actual score − bi. Each binary outcome is estimated using a logit with an intercept and these two score components.

The existing cross-fitted score prediction explains 72.0% of observed score variation (2017: 69.2%; 2019: 74.5%). Baseline predictions and the student sample are unchanged from the linear regressions.

In the logistic specification, the predicted component has the larger association with both outcomes: average-marginal-effect differences of 2.31 percentage points for centralized HE application and 1.51 points for high-premium-field enrollment. The logit coefficient-equality tests give p <0.001 and <0.001, respectively.

Units: average marginal effects in percentage points for a one-unit increase in the indicated component, holding the other component fixed. Both retain the units of the actual score: one within-admission-year standard deviation of the math–verbal composite. They are not standardized separately. Parentheses contain delta-method standard errors from the middle-school clustered covariance; inference uses t with clusters minus one degrees of freedom and is conditional on the existing cross-fitted predictions.

Prediction: five-fold predictions use cubic grade-4 and grade-8 SIMCE math and reading, parental background, middle-school GPA and attendance, and cohort, sex, age, residence-comuna, middle-years-observed and middle-school fixed effects. No high-school identifiers enter. Actual scores refer to timely admission years 2022 (PDT) and 2024 (PAES), standardized within admission year.

Outcomes: centralized HE application is the master data’s regular-application indicator, observed in 2021–2026. High-premium field is the existing indicator at first observed HE enrollment in 2022–2025, with non-enrollment coded zero. Cohort follow-up differs. Both regressions use the same students.

Coverage: 297,397 timely exam takers; 265,911 with both SIMCE8 scores; 234,140 with complete baseline controls. The existing cross-fit excludes 353 students with fixed-effect levels absent from training. No additional students are excluded by the logistic models.

Interpretation: the estimates are conditional associations. The score residual includes all score variation unexplained by measured baseline predictors; it is not a causal estimate of high-school effectiveness. McFadden pseudo-R2 is not a share of outcome variance explained.
