Predicted entrance-exam score and score residual

Grade-8 cohorts 2017 and 2019 · 233,787 exam takers · Five-fold cross-fitted predictions

The observed math–verbal entrance-exam composite is split into its baseline prediction, bi, and the residual, gi = actual score − bi. Each outcome is regressed jointly on these two components and an intercept.

Cross-fitted score prediction explains 72.0% of observed score variation overall (2017: 69.2%; 2019: 74.5%).

Units: percentage-point changes for a one-unit increase in the relevant score component. Both components retain the units of the actual score: one within-admission-year standard deviation of the math–verbal composite. They are not standardized separately. Parentheses contain middle-school clustered standard errors; inference is conditional on the cross-fitted predictions.

Prediction: fifths of students are held out in turn. Predictors include cubic grade-4 and grade-8 SIMCE math and reading scores, parental background, middle-school GPA and attendance, and cohort, sex, age, residence-comuna, middle-years-observed and middle-school fixed effects. No high-school identifiers enter the prediction. Actual entrance scores refer to timely admission years 2022 (PDT) and 2024 (PAES), standardized within admission year.

Outcomes: centralized HE application is the master data’s regular-application indicator, observed in 2021–2026 and derived from its first-application-year variable; it is neither financial-aid application nor application to every IP/CFT. Observed first applications in this sample span 2022–2026. High-premium field is the existing indicator at first observed higher-education enrollment in 2022–2025, with non-enrollment coded zero. Follow-up differs across cohorts. Both regressions use the same students, all with observed SIMCE8 and timely entrance-exam scores.

Coverage: Of 297,397 timely exam takers, 265,911 have both grade-8 SIMCE scores and 234,140 have complete baseline controls. Cross-fitting excludes 353 students whose held-out fixed-effect levels are absent from training; no further students are lost to missing HE outcomes.

Interpretation: these are conditional associations. The residual is everything in the actual score left unexplained by measured baseline predictors; it is not a causal estimate of a high school’s contribution. The second-stage specification contains only an intercept, bi and gi.
